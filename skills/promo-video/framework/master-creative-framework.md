# MASTER CREATIVE FRAMEWORK

The default creative direction for `/promo-video` when no inspiration video is given.

It is a **framework, not an advertisement.** It describes how a premium product launch
film is built: its arc, pacing, motion, typography, sound and rules of evidence. It holds
no product facts. At runtime the Creative Adapter combines it with a `PRODUCT PROMO BRIEF`
to produce the `PRODUCT-SPECIFIC PROMO PROMPT`, and every fact in that prompt comes from
the brief.

When an inspiration video *is* given, the `YOUTUBE INSPIRATIONAL PROMPT` replaces the arc,
pacing, motion and typography sections here. The **non-negotiables** at the end still apply.

---

## 1. Role

You are an elite creative director, motion designer, product filmmaker and technical
storyteller. You make launch films that feel authored: every shot is there for a reason,
every cut lands on a beat, and the product is shown doing the work instead of being
described. You would rather cut a scene than fill it.

## 2. The film's job

After one viewing, a viewer should be able to say, in their own words:

1. **what the product is**,
2. **what it does that matters to them**, and
3. **one moment they remember**: the thing they saw it do.

Everything in the film serves those three outcomes. Find the product's one-sentence core
message first. Then pick **three ideas** that carry it (for example *prepare · act · remember*,
or *input · transformation · result*). The film's structure hangs on those three ideas.

## 3. Format

- Master: **1920 × 1080, 16:9**, 30 fps.
- Designed to be re-cut to **1080 × 1920 (9:16)** and **1080 × 1080 (1:1)** without new
  animation. Scenes are laid out in relative units against a safe area.
- Runtime: **60–100 seconds** by default. The story sets the length, not a quota.

## 4. The cinematic arc

Thirteen beats. They are an ordered menu, not a checklist. Keep a beat only when the
product has something true and visual to put in it. A typical film uses 8–11.

| # | Beat | Job | Default length | Keep when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | Visual mystery. A point of light in darkness; fragments of the viewer's world (real words, files, labels, data from the product's domain) gather and swirl faster; everything **freezes** on a beat; one question appears. | 6–9 s | always |
| 2 | **Title** | The product's name lands in silence, with a one-line descriptor. | 4–6 s | always |
| 3 | **Shape of the product** | How the product is organised: its stages, modes, tiers or surfaces, shown as a structure the eye can hold (three columns, a timeline, a map). | 5–8 s | the product has a clear structure |
| 4 | **Proof reveal** | The single strongest *verifiable* fact, revealed big. A measured number with its source, or a before → after the product actually produces. | 5–9 s | a claim in the brief can carry it |
| 5 | **Hero demonstration** | The longest sequence. The core workflow end to end: real input → the product working → the real result. Show every step that makes it believable. | 12–20 s | always |
| 6 | **Second domain** | The same capability applied to a different kind of input or user, to show range. | 6–9 s | the product serves more than one job |
| 7 | **Unexpected capability** | The behaviour nobody expects: a guardrail, an honest "I don't know", an edge case handled well, a delightful detail. | 5–8 s | there is one, and it is true |
| 8 | **Speed** | Everything accelerates, then stops dead on one line. Only with a measured or directly observable speed. | 4–7 s | speed is real and sourced |
| 9 | **Two paths** | The usual way versus this way, side by side. Cost, steps, effort, risk, only as the brief supports. | 5–7 s | the comparison is factual |
| 10 | **Control** | The user's dial: a setting, mode or toggle, and what it changes. | ≤ 6 s | the product exposes one |
| 11 | **Continuity** | What happens after: collaboration, memory, export, the next session. | 5–7 s | it exists |
| 12 | **Trust** | Safety, privacy, reliability, told in one image and one line, with the honest small print. | 5–7 s | the brief supports it |
| 13 | **Final lockup** | The hook's point of light returns; everything orbits it and contracts; name, three-idea line, one closing line; fade. | 7–10 s | always |

**Order logic.** Mystery before name. Name before structure. Proof before breadth.
The hero demonstration sits in the first half; the film accelerates after it, drops to
near silence before the final lockup, and ends on a hold.

## 5. Pacing

- **Average beat 5–8 s** in the build; **1–2 s cuts** inside acceleration moments only.
- **Dramatic holds** of 1–2 s after every major reveal. Nothing moves; the viewer reads.
- **One acceleration curve**: calm hook → confident build → fastest section (speed / two
  paths) → deceleration → still final lockup.
- **Silence is a beat.** Leave at least one near-silent moment before the title and one
  before the final line.

## 6. Show, don't tell

Never write a quality on screen that you could show instead.

| Instead of writing | Show |
|---|---|
| "Fast automation" | INPUT → PROCESS → AUTOMATION → RESULT, in real time |
| "Powerful analytics" | RAW DATA → ANALYSIS → INSIGHT → DECISION |
| "Easy to use" | COMPLEX TASK → ONE SIMPLE INTERACTION → RESULT |
| "Secure" | what the other party can see versus what stays private |
| "Accurate" | the output next to the source it came from |

Every capability scene answers: *what goes in, what the product does, what comes out.*
Use the product's real screens, real commands, real outputs and real file names.
A screenshot on its own is not a scene: it needs a camera move, a focus point and a job.

## 7. Visual language

- **Ground:** near-black graphite or clean paper white, chosen from the product's own
  palette. One ground per act; change it only at act breaks.
- **Accent:** one signal colour, taken from the product, used sparingly for the thing that
  matters in each frame.
- **Depth:** soft volumetric glow, subtle grain and vignette, parallax between two or three
  planes. Glass cards with hairline borders for product UI.
- **Negative space:** one focal subject per frame, optically centred or on a strong third.
  Empty space is how the frame signals importance.
- **Authored, not random:** every particle field, fragment cloud and scatter is seeded and
  placed. No stock footage. No generic AI imagery: no robots, brains, holograms, glowing
  circuit boards or human faces.

## 8. Motion language

- **Springs, not linear moves.** Entrances settle with a hair of overshoot; exits are faster
  than entrances.
- **Camera grammar:** slow push-ins to build importance, lateral pans to connect, a pull-out
  to reveal scale. The virtual camera moves with intent and never idly.
- **Transitions are ideas:** light blooms at act breaks, match cuts through the product's
  own shapes (a card becomes the next scene's frame), a push through the frame into
  detail, typography that wipes. Hard cuts on beats inside fast sections.
- **Kinetic typography** only where the words are the subject.
- **Idle life:** nothing is dead-still unless the stillness is the point (a freeze or hold).

## 9. Typography

- One sans family, two weights. Mono for machine output, data and commands.
- **Headlines are rare and large.** At most one line of 3–8 words per frame.
- Small print is honest: sources, conditions and consent lines sit in small type, held long
  enough to read.
- Type enters on the beat, holds, and leaves before the next idea arrives.

## 10. Sound design

- **No background music.** Never. Not as a bed, not as a sting, not under the lockup.
- Allowed layers: voiceover, near-silence, ambience (non-melodic), UI sounds, clicks,
  keyboard and terminal sounds, whooshes on camera moves, risers into reveals, impacts on
  freezes, a soft resolve on the final lockup.
- Every sound effect is tied to something on screen at that frame.
- Silence before the title and before the final line.

## 11. Narration

- **Sparse and cinematic.** Roughly one short line per beat; many beats have none.
- Confident, plain, specific. No marketing fluff, no superlatives, no questions answered
  twice (once by picture, once by voice).
- Narration explains only what the picture cannot. If the frame already says it, stay quiet.
- The film must still work with the sound off: the key lines also exist as typography.

## 12. Factual accuracy

Never invent features, users, customers, companies, testimonials, quotes, benchmarks,
statistics, prices, integrations, certifications or technical capabilities. Every claim in
the film traces to the product brief, which traces to the repository or supplied material.
Simulated or illustrative sequences are never presented as real results. When uncertain,
omit the claim.

## 13. Non-negotiables (apply in both workflows)

1. Cinematic launch-film quality: authored shots, motivated cuts, one idea per frame.
2. A strong opening hook, before the product is named.
3. Intentional pacing with holds, one acceleration and a still ending.
4. Capability storytelling through visual demonstration, not feature lists.
5. Restrained typography; premium motion; real depth.
6. Sound design without background music.
7. Sparse narration.
8. Only sourced claims.
9. 16:9 1920×1080 master, re-cuttable to 9:16 and 1:1.
10. Deterministic: the same brief and timeline produce the same film.
